- one x4/x8 vector parallel carry replaces scalar per-lane normalization;
- vector add, subtract, and negate include their required carry/fold pass;
- concrete radix dispatch removes interface-driven hot-path heap escapes;
- fixed three-segment SHA-512 batching hashes the original `R || A || M`

- the canonical-S precheck is allocation-free;

### Post-fusion stage split

The fused build changes the optimization denominator.  On the two-x4,
radix-64 path, a prepared DSM loop takes approximately 7.92 us/signature and
cold A-table construction plus that loop takes approximately 9.02
us/signature.  Complete verification of 64- and 200-byte messages is roughly
17.1 us/signature.  Cold DSM is therefore about 53% of the current complete
path, rather than the 75--80% estimate that applied to an earlier backend.

The paired x4 decoder and its single-A control provide the other crossover
input:

| decode schedule | us/x4 group | us/signature |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A only | 4.184 | 1.046 |
| paired A/R | 6.398 | 1.600 |
| marginal overlapped R | 2.214 | 0.554 |

Consequently, a batch-Q finalizer competes with the measured 0.554
us/signature marginal R cost, not with an entire independent point decode.
The paired decoder remains the likely small-batch winner; the crossover must
be measured in the complete verifier.

### Decoded-A warm tier and paid miss compaction

A private follow-up retains an exact-key decoded `A` point (160 bytes of
coordinates) and bypasses only public-key decompression. It never caches
profile decisions: strict byte checks still run for every signature, the
entry must match all 32 original public-key bytes, and the challenge still
hashes those caller bytes.

The first upper-bound experiment exposed a SIMD granularity problem: one miss
forced an entire original x4 group through decompression, so randomly
interleaved hits saved little. The retained candidate fixes that without
reordering signatures. Within each 64-signature encoder chunk it packs only
decode misses into full x4 calls, decodes them, and scatters the points back to
their original SoA lanes. Hashing, DSM, batch-Q encoding, and verdict mapping
remain in original order.

Pinned-core, three-sample medians for `n=64`, 200-byte messages were:

| decoded-A hits | compact decode groups | pre-resolved (us/sig) | `sync.Map` + batch stats (us/sig) | change vs 16.70 us cold |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0% | 16 | 16.35 | 16.39 | -1.9% |
| 25% | 12 | 16.08 | 16.10 | -3.6% |
| 50% | 8 | 15.79 | 15.82 | -5.3% |
| 75% | 4 | 15.53 | 15.55 | -6.9% |
| 100% | 0 | 15.25 | 15.27 | -8.6% |

The measured steady-state lookup includes one exact `[32]byte` `sync.Map`
load per signature and two aggregate statistic atomics per batch. It adds
roughly 0.1--0.25% at `n=64` and remains zero-allocation. At `n=4`, partial
hits cannot avoid the single decode group, while a 100% hit group still moves
from about 17.1 to 16.0 us/signature. The implementation adds approximately
12.1 KiB of fixed scratch per non-concurrent verifier worker.

This clears the arithmetic and lookup gates, but it is not yet a production
cache. Admission, cache budgets, concurrent workspace pooling, and adversarial
valid-key churn remain outside the measured boundary. The intended admission
rule remains post-verdict: an invalid signature must never create a sighting
or cached point.

### All-cold A-decode phase compaction

Miss compaction is useful even without a cache. The ordinary batch-Q path now
prechecks a complete <=64-signature chunk, packs every surviving A decode into
full x4 calls except the tail, then scatters the decoded points back before
hashing and DSM. This changes only the order of independent public-key
decompressions; equations, hashes, output indexes, and failure atomicity stay
unchanged.

An interleaved A/B on top of the kept selector used ten one-second samples per
side:

| batch | per-two-x4 decode (us/sig) | chunk phase-compacted (us/sig) | change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | 16.82 | 16.23 | -3.51% |
| 8 | 16.39 | 15.81 | -3.57% |
| 64 | 16.09 | 15.49 | -3.73% |

All rows had `p < 0.001`, zero allocations, and passed the complete corpus,
lane/tail, invalid-scatter, and fail-closed gates. This becomes the no-cache
baseline for every later warm-tier comparison.

The earlier decoded-A table remains an upper-bound and lookup experiment, not
a production cache decision. In particular, hit-rate savings must now beat
this faster no-lookup baseline. A first combined benchmark exposed enough
code-layout sensitivity to move absolute rows by several percent, so cache
enablement requires an interleaved same-binary no-lookup versus lookup gate.
Do not infer a TPU policy from the older 16.70 us baseline.

### Per-key micro-AoS x4 selection

The grouped SoA tables above bind four keys to one table object, which prevents
reusing a single key's r51 table when batch membership changes. Commit
`ab14019` adds a private, test-only per-key layout: each 160-byte entry stores
five contiguous `[X,Y,Z,T]` limb rows. One selected entry from each of four
independent key tables is loaded and transposed in registers into the ordinary
`IFMAPointX4` layout.

The standalone leaf performs 20 contiguous YMM loads, 40 shuffles, and 20
stores. It still materializes the selected point; eliminating those stores
would require a separately proven selector/add fusion. Hardware differentials
cover radix 16/32/64, every active mask and signed magnitude, loose u52
representatives, poisoned inactive metadata, invalid-metadata output
atomicity, exact low-level aliasing, and maximum-u52 rows. The first hardware
run also caught and corrected the high-half `VSHUFI64X2` immediate before any
benchmark was accepted.

### Dense affine3 per-key selector

Commit `7047719` tests the three-coordinate affine-cached form needed by a
per-key comb directly: each 120-byte entry stores five packed
`[Y+X,Y-X,2dT]` rows. The final 24-byte row is read with a three-lane masked
YMM load rather than padded back to 32 bytes, retaining the full 25% footprint
reduction. A Linux guard-page test places the entry immediately before an
unreadable page and proves that the masked fourth qword cannot fault. Exact
input/output aliasing, every active/sign mask, u52 boundary limbs,
invalid-metadata output atomicity, and zero allocations also pass on Zen 4.

The first timing run was discarded because another benchmark process was
accidentally pinned to the same core. Serialized one-second samples at 160
entries compared the dense selector with the existing four-coordinate
micro-AoS selector:

| working sets | affine3 payload | full4 payload | signs | affine3 (ns) | full4 (ns) | affine3 change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 75 KiB | 100 KiB | positive | 8.90 | 8.42 | +5.7% |
| 6 | 450 KiB | 600 KiB | positive | 9.00 | 8.26 | +9.0% |
| 1 | 75 KiB | 100 KiB | mixed | 47.13 | 57.29 | -17.7% |
| 6 | 450 KiB | 600 KiB | mixed | 48.33 | 57.29 | -15.6% |

The positive-only path favors naturally aligned 32-byte rows, while the
signed path favors affine3 because it swaps `Y+X/Y-X` and negates only `2dT`;
the extended form negates two field elements. Four-lane scalar recoding rarely
produces an all-positive event, but that probability argument is not a keep
gate. The representation remains experimental until a complete signed point
loop beats the full4 control.

### Asymmetric fixed-B window on the shared chain

Commit `c1be580` keeps A on the current width-6, 43-round micro-AoS path and
changes only the fixed B term. B uses an exact signed window of width 6, 8, 9,
or 10 on the same 252-doubling accumulator chain; width 10 has 26 rounds and a
single 512-entry, 60 KiB affine table. The evaluator preserves exact integer
`-k` semantics for mixed-order A and tests all masks, boundary digits,
noncanonical scalar rejection, fail-closed output, and zero allocations.

Three serialized, pinned-core one-second samples were tightly clustered:

or production-dispatch gate.

### In-loop comb doubling/add cadence

Commit `d32a6a7` measures the exact short dependency shape raised by the
partial-comb analysis: `D^w` followed by one or two already-selected affine
cached additions. Selection, sign application, and table construction are
outside the timed region. Three one-second samples were effectively identical:

| cadence | ns/block |
| --- | ---: |
| six doubles, no add (continuous-chain reference) | 579.2 |
| one affine cached add | 106.8 |
| two affine cached adds | 212.9 |
| six doubles plus one add | 684.8 |
| six doubles plus two adds | 790.8 |

Thus the inserted in-cadence doubling cost is
`(684.8 - 106.8) / 6 = 96.3 ns`. The base addition is 106.8 ns and the local
second addition is `790.8 - 684.8 = 106.0 ns`, giving `C_D/C_A` of 0.902 and
0.909 by the two subtraction methods. Both remain far below the 1.324
dominance crossover, so the proposed A4/r2 arm is dropped and A6/r9 remains
the next per-key comb candidate. This decision is kernel-specific and must be
rerun after a fused double, dedicated square, or fused affine-add change.

### Heterogeneous partial A/B comb

Commit `a8040f2` implements the first exact, test-only composition of the
per-key A comb with an independently tuned fixed-B table. Both candidates use
one merged accumulator and exactly 48 online doublings:

- A is width 6 with nine passes (`A6/r9`) in both candidates;
- the smaller B arm is width 8 with three passes (`B8/r3`); and
- the wider B arm is width 10 with five passes (`B10/r5`).

The evaluator recodes the A multiplier as the exact signed integer `-k`, not
`L-k`, and uses dense 120-byte affine3 entries for both terms. Independent
Edwards-reference tests exercise mixed-order A and B, all active masks, the
last valid A/B cells, `2^252-1` and `2^252`, shifted half-radix carries,
simultaneous A/B injections at exponents zero and 30, invalid top cells,
invalid scalar fail-closed behavior, and zero allocations. The complete r51x5
suite passes on the Ryzen hardware.

Ten serialized, pinned-core, three-second samples measured the prepared DSM
loop:

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| regular A6 + dense B10 control | 20 KiB/group | 60 KiB | 38.372 | 9.593 | control |
| partial A6/r9 + B8/r3 | 75 KiB/group (18.75 KiB/key) | 165 KiB | 17.905 | 4.476 | -53.3% |
| partial A6/r9 + B10/r5 | 75 KiB/group (18.75 KiB/key) | 360 KiB | 16.794 | about 4.20 | -56.2% |

This clears the online-arithmetic gate and confirms that per-key comb and r51

This clears the construction-cost microbenchmark gate, but it is not yet a

Commit `710b452` adds a test-only alternative to the fixed-B selector. The

### Unchecked no-alias x8 table selection

The same redundant-copy issue remained in the dormant x8 path: every selected
point built a 1,280-byte identity, gathered lanes into that temporary, and
copied the entire point to the non-aliasing local destination. A private x8
helper now writes through and initializes only zero or inactive lanes. It is
used only by internally recoded DSM, variable-base, and HEEA workspaces. The
exported caller-owned-digit loop and public selector retain validation and
alias safety.

The x8 differential mirrors the x4 boundary suite over radix 16/32/64,
reduced and deliberately loose tables, every one of 256 active masks, signed
boundary digits, garbage-prefilled outputs, and poisoned inactive metadata.
An isolated complete-verifier A/B used identical sources and tests except for
the private helper body:

| A radix (`n=8`) | copied selector (us/sig) | direct no-alias (us/sig) | change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 | 18.52 | 18.05 | -2.54% |
| 32 | 17.05 | 16.74 | -1.85% |
| 64 | 16.91 | 16.43 | -2.84% |

All rows were statistically significant (`p <= 0.009`), zero-allocation, and
the geomean improvement was about 2.4%.

A separate radix-64 tail probe covered every active width from one through
eight lanes. No median regressed: `n=2/4/5/8` improved significantly by about
1.2--2.1%, while `n=1/3/6/7` were statistically neutral in the short
five-sample gate. Wider counts through 17 plus 32/64 likewise showed either a
gain or no significant change. The direct helper therefore needs no
occupancy-dependent fallback.

A new dense full-lane selector benchmark also separates the old costs. At
radix 64 the checked path was 206.9 ns, removing digit validation alone was
203.4 ns, direct output while retaining whole-point identity initialization
was 183.2 ns, and initializing only lanes that need identity was 159.3 ns.
Radix 16/32 gave the same shape. Validation is negligible; the final copy and
whole-point initialization each cost about 20--24 ns.

Dense selection remains about 159--160 ns at every tested radix despite a
fourfold table-footprint increase. This predictable one-table benchmark prices
copying accurately but cannot settle cache behavior under irregular two-table
DSM access, so it does not justify a lane-contiguous table/transpose rewrite
by itself. A ten-second Go CPU profile of complete x8 radix-64 verification
attributes about 15% cumulative time to selection, split primarily between
public lane gathering and signed point normalization, so selection remains
worth improving through cached point formulas or fusion. Privileged PMU
counters were unavailable because the host has `perf_event_paranoid=4`; no
L1/L2 claim is made from the source layout alone.

### Y-first strict finalizer

A dormant alternative retains the exact literal `Encode(Q)==Rbytes`
predicate but checks projective Y first. Canonical low255(R) is imported,
`Y_Q == y_R*Z_Q` is tested modulo p, and only surviving lanes enter the
cross-group inversion needed for canonical affine-X parity. An all-Y-mismatch
batch therefore avoids the inversion entirely. Literal batch encoding remains
the default and differential oracle.

The hardware/model differential covers canonical and noncanonical R,
`R=-Q`, identity/order-two negative-zero aliases, non-unit projective Z,
leading empty groups, tails, injected failures, and zero-Z fail-closed paths.
The complete verifier additionally matches CCTV, Wycheproof, Firedancer
regressions, both profiles, every invalid lane, and chunk boundaries. All
tested paths allocate zero memory.

Complete 200-byte results were:

| batch | valid share | literal batch Q (us/sig) | y-first (us/sig) | approximate change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | 0% | 16.65 | 15.68 | -5.8% |
| 4 | 100% | 16.58 | 16.51 | -0.4% |
| 8 | 0% | 16.21 | 15.68 | -3.2% |

Intermediate valid shares were neutral to modestly positive. This is not a

### Coordinate-parallel x4 singleton experiment

The ordinary r51 layout assigns one signature to each x4 lane, so a singleton
still pays nearly a full group. A test-only alternative reinterprets the same
four independent field lanes as one point's `[X,Y,T,Z]` coordinates. Its
doubling schedule computes `[X^2,Y^2,Z^2,XY]` in one fused x4 multiply,
normalizes one packed Edwards intermediate, and computes all four output
coordinates in a second fused multiply. It reuses the proven radix-51 field
kernel and composable range contract but remains a distinct point execution
mode, not production dispatch.

Ten pinned-core, one-second samples on the 8700GE were stable:

| chained point double | ns/op | allocations |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| coordinate-parallel packed x4 | 58.78 | 0 |
| current signature-parallel x4 with one live lane | 129.6 | 0 |

The matching cached/Niels addition must normalize both `[Y-X,Y+X,T,Z]` and
the later `[E,G,H,F]` factors; omitting either carry boundary is not sound for
the generic u52 domain. Even with both normalizations it measured 56.89 ns,
versus 140.6 ns for the current one-live-lane extended-point addition, a 2.47x
gain.

A point-arithmetic gate then ran the exact radix-64 loop shape: 43 digit
rounds, six doubles between rounds (252 total), and two cached additions per
round. It intentionally excludes scalar recoding, table selection/build,
decode, hashing, and finalization:

| radix-64 point loop | us/op | allocations |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| coordinate-parallel packed x4 | 20.13 | 0 |
| current signature-parallel x4 with one live lane | 40.78 | 0 |

The packed loop is approximately 2.03x faster. Hardware/model/scalar
differentials cover mixed-order points, random non-unit projective `Z`,
identity and low-order torsion, aliasing, deliberately corrupted input `T`,
and the generic u52 range envelope. This is a point-operation result rather
than a complete-verifier claim. Cold singleton verification may still lose to

### Coordinate-parallel NAF DSM

The first complete packed DSM experiment exposed an important mismatch in the
initial singleton projection. The 20.13-us point-loop row above uses two
already-selected addends on every radix-64 round. It is not comparable to the
generic or curve25519-voi singleton verifier, both of which use a sparse
width-5 NAF for arbitrary `A`, a width-8 NAF for fixed `B`, only eight odd
`A` multiples, and a process-wide fixed-base table.

The packed radix-64 DSM measured 19.46 us with both tables prepared and 25.69
us when rebuilding `A`'s table. Those rows still exclude decoding, hashing,
scalar reduction, strict preparation, and finalization. Adding the measured
4.184-us one-group `A` decoder already puts the current cold path above 29 us
before those remaining stages. A 22--26-us complete-verifier projection is
therefore not supported by the measured components.

Commit `f903305` adds a test-only schedule with eight positive odd `A`
multiples (width 5), 64 positive odd fixed-`B` multiples (width 8), and exact
integer `-k` semantics for mixed-order public keys. Ten pinned-core,
two-second samples compare it with the packed radix-64 control:

| packed DSM | radix-64 | width-5/width-8 NAF | delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| prepared tables | 20.30 us | 20.05 us | -1.24% |
| cold `A` table | 25.64 us | 22.72 us | -11.39% |

Both deltas are significant at `p=0.000` (`n=10`) and every row remains at
zero allocations. The hardware path agrees with the Go model and an
independent Edwards reference over zero, `l-1`, random canonical scalars, and
a mixed-order `A`; noncanonical scalar input fails closed.

This makes coordinate-parallel NAF the singleton candidate to carry into a
complete strict pipeline. Even after the 11.39% DSM win, `A` decode plus the
cold DSM is about 26.9 us, so the full-path gate must be measured before
claiming parity with curve25519-voi or Firedancer. Raw output is in
`results/narya-zen4-2026-07-24/narya-quad-naf-tests.txt`,

### Direct-XY signature-parallel doubling

A range/carry review initially estimated a roughly threefold gap between the
measured verifier and an arithmetic throughput floor. That comparison assumed
an already fused `4S+4M` doubling with minimal carry placement. The actual
two-x4 candidate does materially more work: there is no dedicated squaring
kernel, so every square is the same 25-product multiply, and every composable
add, subtract, and negate performs its own normalization. The historical
square-trick signature-parallel doubling had twelve normalization passes
before its four output products, rather than the proposed five at depth two.
The current direct-`XY` schedule has ten before its four output products, or
fourteen total. The full extended-point addition has fourteen before its four
output products, or eighteen total. Static assembly counting prices the
historical prepared radix-64 loop at roughly 500,500 vector instructions per
x4 group; the current direct-`XY` loop is roughly 480,300, before selection,
decode, hashing, reduction, and finalization. The purported unexplained
threefold gap therefore does not exist for this code.

The audit did identify a local algebraic win. The previous formula computed
`E=2XY` as `(X+Y)^2-X^2-Y^2`. Since the current backend has no cheaper square,
computing `XY` directly keeps the multiplication count unchanged and removes
two normalized field operations. The old square-trick implementation remains
an independently timed test control.

Pinned-core primitive chains separated dependent latency from four-chain
throughput. Values below are nanoseconds per x4 primitive:

| primitive | dependent | independent-4 throughput |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| fused multiply plus normalize | 10.40 | 7.235 |
| square via the general multiply | 11.35 | 7.171 |
| standalone u61-to-u52 normalize | 3.389 | 2.123 |
| add plus normalize | 3.607 | 2.619 |

The direct point A/B measured 117.6 ns for the square trick and 107.5 ns for
direct `XY`, an 8.6% improvement with zero allocations. A ten-sample,
two-second complete-verifier comparison at `n=64`, 200-byte messages, and the
phase-compacted no-cache batch-Q path measured:

| doubling formula | ns/batch | us/signature | allocations |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| square trick | 1,077,000 | 16.83 | 0 |
| direct `XY` | 1,024,000 | 16.00 | 0 |

Benchstat reports `-4.89% sec/op` and `-4.93% us/signature`, both with
`p=0.000`, `n=10`. Short matching runs at `n=4` and `n=8` improved by about
5.5% and 6.3% respectively. Hardware/model differentials, repeated mixed-order
doubling chains, the u52 range gate, aliasing, and the zero-allocation gate all
pass.

The absolute values above came from the cache-lookup benchmark call site,
whereas the earlier 15.49-us best used the original complete batch-Q gate.
This code has measurable layout/call-site sensitivity, so the exact historical
row was also rerun on both worktrees:

| batch | square trick (us/sig) | direct `XY` (us/sig) | change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |

| 64 | 16.20 | 15.46 | -4.57% |

The directly comparable pre-copy-removal result was therefore 15.46
us/signature, not 16.00. The older 15.49 result and 15.46 result are

The current production point layer still implements every square by calling

### Eliminate the defensive x4 point copy

Disassembly of the x4 composable doubling core exposed a 640-byte
`qq := *q` copy before every double. The result is held in a separate local
point and assigned to `out` only after all input reads complete, so the copy
is not required even when `out == q`. Removing it changes no arithmetic or
representation contract.

The isolated point A/B measured approximately 107.2 ns for the copied core
and 96.3 ns for the otherwise identical no-copy core, a roughly 10.1% point
double improvement. In the prepared radix-64 loop (252 doubles and 86 adds),
the same change measured 38.55 us to 37.14 us, about 3.7%. Removing the two
analogous point-add input copies looked strong in isolation but did not
improve the complete loop, so the production change is deliberately limited
to x4 doubling.

Two clean temporary worktrees based at `64851dc` then compared the complete
single-A, cross-group batch-Q verifier. Six two-second samples on the pinned
Ryzen core produced:

| batch | before (us/sig) | no-copy (us/sig) | benchstat change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | 16.27 | 15.41 | -5.29% |
| 8 | 15.79 | 15.08 | -4.50% |
| 64 | 15.55 | 14.78 | -4.95% |

All three comparisons report `p=0.002`, `n=6`, zero allocations; the sec/op
geomean improves by 4.89%. `go test ./... -count=1` passes on the Zen 4 host.
The production checkpoint is `0877264`. Raw results, benchstat output, the
full test log, and source provenance are in the matching
`narya-nocopy-*` artifacts.

The widened Stage-2 experiment remains test-only. Its linear/carry leaf is
fast in isolation, but four raw Stage-1 calls plus a 640-byte workspace round
trip measured 38.42 us for the same point loop, only about 0.35% better than
the copied baseline and slower than no-copy production. Adding a
symmetry-aware raw square improved that candidate to 37.44 us (about 2.9%),
still slower than the simpler 37.14-us no-copy loop. The next fusion
experiment must therefore join Stage 1 and Stage 2 in one leaf; merely moving
the carry boundaries across a memory ABI is not enough.

### Complete the x8 double-copy cleanup

Commit `8a048c1` applies the same alias proof to the dormant x8 static
doubler and to the two injected model/oracle variants. On Go 1.26.4
linux/amd64, the old x8 symbol copied 1,280 input bytes before every double and
used a 5,144-byte stack frame. The new symbol has no input copy and uses a
3,880-byte frame. Output remains failure-atomic because all input reads finish
before the separately built result is assigned to the caller.

Order-reversed primitive runs on the pinned Ryzen measured 306.3 ns for the
old public x8 double and about 290.9 ns after the change, approximately -5.0%,
with zero allocations. A source-level same-binary legacy duplicate was not
accepted as stronger evidence because Go optimized its redundant local copy
away, making both benchmark arms identical. Separate-binary complete-pipeline
runs also landed in different wall-clock frequency clusters and contradicted
the primitive direction, so no complete-verifier speedup is claimed from that
artifact. Hardware cycle counters are unavailable to the unprivileged Ryzen
account (`perf_event_paranoid=4`).

This cleanup matters primarily to the retained x8/Zen 5 candidate; Zen 4
continues to prefer two x4 groups. The injected variants are testing
infrastructure rather than hot production code. Static point-add copies remain
unchanged because their earlier complete-loop A/B did not improve performance.

The width-aware design improves the cold one-signature case by roughly 30%

Oasis expanded-key construction is approximately 3.9 us and stores about
1.5 KiB.  Narya's generic full `PubkeyTable` already implements a 32-by-8
pre-shifted arbitrary-key comb using 30,720 bytes.  A future r51 hot-key table
must therefore be compared with both of these existing cache tiers rather than
treated as a new algorithm in isolation.

## Decisions and next experiments

1. Keep the vector parallel carry and the x4-first Zen 4 direction.
2. Keep fused raw multiplication plus parallel carry; it improved complete x4
   verification by approximately 11–13% across the release-shaped matrix.
3. Keep single-A decode plus native cross-group batch encoding of computed
   `Q`; it improves complete verification by about 3% at n=4 and 5--7% at
   n=8--64, with the gain preserved through eight physical cores.
4. Keep the private unchecked/no-alias selectors only in internally recoded
   workspaces. The x4 helper improves complete batch-Q verification by
   3.1--3.5%; the x8 helper improves complete x8 verification by about 2.4%
   geomean. Checked selectors remain the public safety boundaries and oracles.
5. Keep all-cold chunk-local A-decode phase compaction; it improves complete
   batch-Q verification by another 3.5--3.7% with zero allocations.
6. Retain the exact-key decoded-A tier as an experiment, but re-gate it against
   the faster no-lookup phase-compacted path before production wiring. Cache
   admission remains gated on workload and valid-key-churn traces.
7. Keep y-first dormant for small invalid-heavy ingress; literal batch-Q stays
   the valid-likely/replay default.
8. Next consider fused point add/double or a radix-64 scalar-loop
   superkernel.  Any older `DBL5+ADD` proposal must be adapted to the measured
   radix-64/x4 winner.
9. Treat raw-coordinate, carry-on-consume, and operand-resident multiplication
   as scheduling techniques inside those fused assembly kernels.  Do not
   expose a general Wide63/u64 field contract without a separate proof and
   differential boundary tests.
10. Evaluate a shared r51 hot-key comb first for shred/validator-key traffic,
   where all x4 lanes can naturally share `A`.  Use measured reuse distance and
   build amortization; do not infer it from the accounts cache.
